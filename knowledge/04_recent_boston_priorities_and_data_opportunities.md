# Boston's recent priorities → Civic project and data opportunities

Research cutoff: **October 24, 2026, Eastern Time.** Prepared for Hackachusetts Civic Action. Read alongside the [event/rules playbook](01_rules_judging_and_event_playbook.md), [stakeholders and full dataset inventory](02_civic_strategy_stakeholders_and_ideas.md), and [past winners](03_past_winners_and_patterns.md).

This brief connects public statements by Boston City Hall, agencies, community partners, and event stakeholders to feasible uses of the hackathon datasets. **Yes, this is a useful way to choose the project:** identify a documented problem, check whether the data can answer it, and demonstrate a useful next action. Public priorities are evidence of relevance, not proof of judges' private preferences or an endorsement.

## 1. Decision first

**Best default with the supplied data: a neighborhood service evidence-to-action workflow.** Use a defined 311 time window to help a resident organization or staff reviewer understand one service category, inspect the supporting records/aggregates, and prepare a sourced follow-up brief. The differentiator must be the completed workflow and trustworthy analysis; Boston already has open-data AI tooling.

**Strong alternative: transit access to one essential destination.** A resident sees relevant disruptions and official next steps for reaching a campus, training provider, or civic service. This connects to recent transportation and workforce priorities. Choose it only if route/stop coverage and current feeds can be validated; the starter rail-route query is not an all-transit dataset.

**Reconsider a generic permit chatbot.** Housing and permitting are prominent concerns, but a City AI beta already translates residents' tasks into permit experiences. A useful new entry would need a specific, observed completion gap, such as a source-checked preparation packet with explicit jurisdiction and missing information. [N8][N12]

Our ranking reflects fit, data availability, distinctiveness, and one-day scope. It is not an official scoring model. We have not interviewed City staff or validated demand with residents.

### A. Boston 311: quickest useful analytical foundation

- **Portal source resource:** `254adca6-64ab-4c5c-9fc0-a6da622be185`.
- **Starter selected fields:** `open_date`, `case_topic`, `service_name`, `assigned_department`, `case_status`, `neighborhood`, `zip_code`, `on_time`, `city_council_district`, `ward`.
- **Source probe:** [CKAN schema request, zero records][D2]. This link is a proposed inspection step; it did not return usable data in this research session.
- **Population context:** resource `b0543358-d03f-4682-bf0c-658ea4573d6f`; selected fields include `name`, `year`, `population_b01001_001e`, and poverty-related counts. Match neighborhoods through an explicit crosswalk and preserve unmatched rows; ZIP codes, wards and neighborhoods are different boundaries. [D1]
- **Schema-change lead:** Garces' public repository documents an October 2025 311 migration, changing fields such as `case_enquiry_id` to `case_id`, `open_dt` to `open_date`, and `closed_dt` to `close_date`. This is an author-maintained technical reference, not a schema response observed today. Inspect the actual feed before implementing it; don't concatenate old and new records by position. [D8]

**Proposed first question:** “For this service category and this complete time window, what do these records show in the selected neighborhood, and what should a human reviewer investigate next?”

**Calculations:** use deterministic code/SQL for counts and dates; AI explains the returned aggregates. If computing share marked on time, document the recognized values and denominator. Keep null/unknown separate. If computing age, distinguish age since opening from time overdue. The latter requires a validated target date. A closed case is not necessarily evidence that the resident's problem was solved.

**Sampling constraint:** the portal advertises a bounded starter extract. Calculate its min/max dates, row count, and coverage before drawing conclusions. A latest-N-record sample cannot support a citywide rate comparison without a defensible sampling design. Even with complete records, 311 measures reported requests, not all unmet need; duplicate reporting, service mix, channel access and staffing can affect comparisons.

**Concrete output:** an accessible one-page brief with time window, coverage note, aggregate chart/table, source link, uncertainty, and draft questions for the responsible department. A person reviews/export it. Do not send requests or assert department fault automatically.

### B. Transit access: strong current relevance, more integration work

- [MBTA alerts endpoint][D3] and [starter route query][D4] are public source links from the event portal.
- The starter route query uses `filter[type]=0,1`; it must not be described as all buses and rail.
- Match native route/stop IDs from validated responses. Station names alone are fragile join keys. A station point and a nearby destination are not proof of a walkable or accessible connection.
- Review the [MBTA V3 reference][D5] before adding bus routes, stops, schedules or facilities. Live response freshness and API behavior remain untested here.
- The City announcement explicitly says some Link Bus routes/stops/schedules are not finalized. Keep future Link Bus plans in a separate “planned service” layer. [N5]

**Proposed one-day scope:** one known destination, a small set of verified existing routes, the relevant current alerts, a plain-language explanation with official links, and a visible “last fetched” time. If elevator/path data is missing, say accessibility is unknown; do not invent an accessible alternative. A cached fallback must be labeled with its timestamp.

**Differentiation:** explain the effect of a disruption on a specific resident task, with a useful official handoff. A general route planner needs far more data and competes with established tools.

### C. Housing: prominent need, strong temptation to overclaim

- **Permit resource:** `6ddcd912-32a0-43df-9908-63574f8c7e77`; [schema probe][D6], not successfully read here.
- **Starter fields:** `permitnumber`, `worktype`, `permittypedescr`, `description`, `declared_valuation`, `total_fees`, `issued_date`, `expiration_date`, `status`, `occupancytype`, `sq_feet`, `zip`, `ward`. [D1]
- The starter does not select parcel IDs, applicant-submission timestamps, step-level processing events or rejection reasons. A richer source may contain more fields; inspect instead of assuming.
- Join to code violations only after finding a valid shared property identifier or carefully validated location relationship. Aggregation by ZIP is area context, not a property-level match or evidence that a violation caused a permit delay.
- `declared_valuation` is not rent, affordability, assessed value, or realized construction cost. Permit count is not housing-unit count. Issuance is not completed construction.

**Viable output:** a historical activity explorer plus a distinct, source-maintained preparation checklist for one task. Avoid a “permit delay predictor” trained on issued-only records. The competing City AI beta makes usability testing and a specific unmet need essential. [N12]

### D. Opportunity pathways: connect three different kinds of evidence

Use **institution evidence** (Scorecard/IPEDS), **occupation evidence** (O*NET), and a **current program directory** as separate tables. Where present and validated, `UNITID` is the natural Scorecard–IPEDS institutional key. Occupation-to-program matching needs an explicit reviewed mapping; a language model's similarity score does not establish that a credential qualifies someone for a job.

The October 20 Pathmaker announcement opens applications for **training providers partnering with employers**. It does not mean every resident can enroll that day. Separate `funding_application`, `resident_enrollment`, `information_only`, and `not_yet_open` states. [N2]

**One-day output:** a comparison of a few verified options, prerequisites as published, contact/next-step links and source dates. Add a route or campus context only if it is reliable. Avoid eligibility decisions and employment promises.

### E. Coastal resilience: preparation and participation, not a hazard model

The event provides [FEMA Massachusetts declaration history][D7] and an environmental-justice extract, while Coastmix already addresses interactive coastal design. A complementary proposal is a **neighborhood preparedness/evidence packet** that links local official plans, explains relevant terminology, and helps a community group formulate questions.

Before building a map of who is exposed, obtain actual flood scenario polygons and geographic identifiers. The portal's EJ query omits geometry and selects a bounded set of records. It cannot identify a household's risk. Do not use disaster declarations as current warnings or derive evacuation instructions from an LLM. [D1]

## 5. A database structure that preserves the evidence

This is a proposed model for the eventual app, not a database already connected. Keep public-priority research distinct from service records and operational rules. A mayoral statement helps establish why a problem matters; it does not change the requirements of a permit or the meaning of a dataset field.

| Table | Suggested fields | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `public_sources` | `source_id`, `url`, `publisher`, `title`, `published_at`, `event_at`, `retrieved_at`, `evidence_type`, `review_status` | Preserve dates, provenance and whether we reviewed full text, a summary or a recording |
| `priority_statements` | `statement_id`, `source_id`, `stakeholder`, `role_at_statement_date`, `topic`, `geographic_scope`, `paraphrase`, `status` | Store concise paraphrases; distinguish announced, proposed, operating and historical |
| `datasets` | `dataset_id`, `publisher`, `source_url`, `resource_id`, `snapshot_at`, `coverage_start`, `coverage_end`, `schema_version`, `license_url`, `validation_status` | Separate live sources from supplied snapshots and record actual coverage |
| `priority_dataset_links` | `statement_id`, `dataset_id`, `question_supported`, `join_method`, `missing_fields`, `claim_not_supported` | Make the problem-to-data connection explicit and reviewable |
| `ingestion_runs` | `run_id`, `dataset_id`, `fetched_at`, `row_count`, `min_event_date`, `max_event_date`, `error`, `content_hash` | Show what was actually retrieved and detect stale or partial imports |
| Domain tables | Typed 311, permit, institution or transit records, each carrying `dataset_id` and `run_id` | Query real records without treating generated prose as data |
| `analysis_outputs` | `output_id`, `query_parameters`, `source_run_ids`, `calculation_version`, `generated_at`, `review_state` | Reproduce the numbers behind the explanation |

Store a short paraphrase and link for news/interviews, not full copyrighted articles. Treat retrieved text as untrusted input. Keep calculations, source identifiers and uncertainty separate from generated explanations. Do not needlessly collect residents' personal information to demonstrate an aggregate public-data workflow.

**Example evidence chain:** `N10: resident-centered services` → `D2: 311 snapshot` → `question: category volume within known coverage` → `deterministic count` → `human-reviewed brief`. The statement supports relevance; the dataset supports the count. Neither alone proves causal impact.

### First 30 minutes of data validation

1. Pick one user, one question and at most two operational datasets.
2. Download the organizer's [Civic starter kit][D9] and inspect its actual files, schemas and metadata. This research reviewed the catalog but did not inspect the ZIP contents.
3. Confirm row count, date range, missing values, category values, duplicate handling and geographic coverage. Preserve raw files and record source URLs.
4. Try one bounded request against each required live API. Record response status, schema and retrieval time; don't quietly swap a snapshot into a “live” label.
5. Verify one join with a small sample. Keep unmatched rows visible. Use a reviewed crosswalk when geographic boundaries differ.
6. Manually calculate one result and compare it with the app's result. Create one meaningful regression check around a real failure risk: missing dates, stale alerts, changed categories, or mismatched geography.
7. Cut any feature whose essential data cannot be verified. A truthful working slice is stronger than an unsupported citywide claim.

## 6. What to show judges

Connect four pieces in order: **a dated public problem → a specific user → relevant records → a completed next step**. Cite one short current source in the problem framing and keep full links in the README/submission. Do not spend the video on a tour of stakeholder news.

Example framing, to adapt only after the product works:

> Boston's current service agenda emphasizes everyday resident tasks. Our prototype helps a neighborhood organization turn a defined 311 extract into a source-backed brief, with clear coverage and a human-reviewed next step.

This is proposed narration, not an actual impact claim. Replace it with the team's precise user and measured behavior. If a trial takes a user from eight manual steps to four, show that result and the trial conditions; do not extrapolate to citywide cost savings.

**Kiro footage:** include about **20–30 seconds of your own build evidence** after the product demo. Show a real spec criterion, the Kiro feature/action that helped implement it, and the verified result. A useful example would be a stale-data requirement becoming a visible warning and a passing check. Keep the complete submission video under three minutes and retain the required written Kiro usage account and root `/.kiro` artifacts. See [the detailed 2:50 storyboard](01_rules_judging_and_event_playbook.md#10-a-250-demo-structure). This allocation is our recommendation; Kiro footage itself was not separately mandated in the rules we read.

**What the current evidence does not establish:** a hidden judging preference, guaranteed City adoption, a funded partnership with our team, access to internal databases, or permission to submit requests to government systems. Public relevance and a feasible pilot are useful claims; endorsements require actual agreement.
