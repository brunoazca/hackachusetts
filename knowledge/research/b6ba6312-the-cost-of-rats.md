## Cycle 001 — Peer-reviewed study on rodents & housing prices?

**Sub-question:** Is there any peer-reviewed hedonic or disclosure study linking rodents to housing prices or rents?

**Evidence strength:** strong (multiple independent searches converge)

1. **No peer-reviewed study** measures rodents/rats vs home prices or rents. Searches surface only forest/tree-pest hedonic work. [nearest hedonic example](https://www.mdpi.com/1999-4907/10/9/820)
2. The "5–20% value loss from pests" figure is **industry/marketing** — US pest-control company blogs (Zunex, Thrasher, NestApple) + tabloids, attributed loosely to NPMA, not research. [example](https://thrasherpestcontrol.com/how-pest-issues-affect-real-estate/)
3. Nearest **peer-reviewed** evidence: forest-pest hedonic studies (emerald ash borer, sudden oak death) find ~**2–5%** discounts on nearby homes — about trees, not rodents in dwellings, so not a valid proxy. [USDA FS](https://www.fs.usda.gov/psw/publications/documents/psw_gtr229/psw_gtr229_154.pdf)

**Pitch verdict:** The "rats knock 10–20% off a home" claim is an **industry claim, not a study**. Honest line: no academic study has quantified rodents' effect on home prices; the closest peer-reviewed work (tree pests) shows only ~2–5%.

## Cycle 002 — Pathogens in Boston rats (CORRECTION: Tufts, not BU)

**Sub-question:** What did researchers find about pathogens in Boston rats?

**Evidence strength:** strong (peer-reviewed PLOS NTD + corroborated by AAU, ScienceDaily, Boston Globe)

The study is by **Tufts University** (with Northern Arizona University), PI Marieke Rosenbaum DVM — published in **PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases**. The brief said "Boston University"; that is incorrect.

1. **18% of Boston brown rats (59 of 328)** across **17 city areas** carried *Leptospira* bacteria (leptospirosis), by PCR of kidney samples. [One Health summary](https://www.aaha.org/trends-magazine/publications/leptospirosis-in-urban-rats-a-one-health-study/)
2. A **Boston-area human leptospirosis case** had a strain genetically near-identical to a local rat population — highly suggestive of rat→human transmission. [Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/06/20/metro/rats-boston-disease-leptospirosis-tufts/)
3. Authors warn **eradication can spread** new strains between rat groups as survivors relocate; they recommend **sanitation over extermination**. [AAU](https://www.aau.edu/newsroom/urban-rats-spread-deadly-bacteria-they-migrate-study)

**Pitch value:** strongest peer-reviewed *health* number — "18% of Boston rats carry the leptospirosis bacterium, and a human case was genetically linked to city rats."

## Cycle 004 — NYC & Chicago rodent programs: cost + results

**Sub-question:** What have NYC rat mitigation zones and Chicago's rodent program published on cost and measured results?

**Evidence strength:** strong (NYC decline corroborated by nyc.gov + silive; Chicago by Orkin + AP/city audit)

**NYC (government):**
1. 311 rat sightings down for **12 straight months, >20% YoY** — credited to trash containerization. [DSNY](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dsny/news/25-039/nyc-rats-fleeing-an-incredible-12-straight-months-)
2. **55%** drop in the Hamilton Heights containerization pilot zone. [NYC report](https://a860-gpp.nyc.gov/downloads/t435gh62m?locale=en)
3. **$877,000** to rat-proof ~600,000 street tree beds. [Mayor's Office](https://www.nyc.gov/mayors-office/news/2025/06/mayor-adams-launches-new-teams-fight-rats-city-s-new-phase-war-rats)
4. Citywide complaints down **~18% YoY** (10,139→8,322, Jan–May). [SILive / city data](https://www.silive.com/data/2025/06/nyc-winning-its-war-on-rats-as-complaints-drop-in-nearly-every-borough-data-shows.html)
5. NYC has a mayor-appointed **"rat czar" / Office of Rodent Mitigation**.

**Chicago (industry + govt audit):**
6. Perennial Orkin **"rattiest city"** (#1 for years, now #2 behind LA) — an **industry** ranking from Orkin's own treatment counts. A **2026 city Inspector General audit** found Chicago's program reactive and "unlikely to achieve long-term control." [AP](https://apnews.com/article/chicago-rat-control-audit-complaints-root-causes-c2e1e14891b705480deb38e561d18ca0)

**Pitch value:** NYC proves **containerization works** (measured ~20% / 55% drops) — the model BRAP follows. Chicago is the cautionary tale (reactive program, audit says it won't work long-term).
