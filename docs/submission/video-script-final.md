## Editing direction

- **One voice for the whole film.** Record the narration in short sections, with the same mic position. Speak as a founder explaining a useful tool. No long spoken introductions or reading teammates' names.
- **One restrained music bed.** A short rising phrase gives the folklore opening scale. Lower it under the demo. Bring the opening motif back for the final logo. Keep clicks subtle and omit repetitive whooshes.
- **Use continuous screen recordings as the foundation.** Record the core path first, then cut pauses and load times. Preserve click → actual result. Do not claim elapsed-time savings from an edited sequence.
- **Move the camera only to help someone read.** Start with the whole app, then crop to one panel. Use one readable close-up at a time and let it settle before another action. Do not stretch 1080p footage through large zooms.
- **Keep the same place.** The audience should recognize its name in the list, detail panel, and downloaded brief. A changing queue rank is irrelevant; never rely on it being the first row.
- **Show one action per shot.** Let the map finish moving before reading a panel. Avoid fast cursor circles and scrolling while narrating a number.
- **Subtitle the real recorded voice.** Two lines maximum, placed clear of controls and source labels. Use short editorial overlays sparingly; the app already contains information to read.
- **No extra full-screen transitions during the demo.** The five-second bridge is the one explanatory interruption. Small labels such as “The history” and “The field brief” may sit over real footage for two seconds.
- **Export 2:58.** Confirm the duration on the encoded file and watch it at the size judges will see. The final logo hold is included in that runtime.
